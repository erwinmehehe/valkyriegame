# Science Lab — Curriculum Foundation

## World structure

Science Lab uses the established world sequence:

**Greenhouse -> Weather Tower -> Creature Grove**

Milo is the observation companion. His role is to inspect, notice, listen, compare, and prompt predictions rather than simply reveal answers.

## Learning model

Science Lab teaches science through physical investigation:

- observe before choosing
- predict before testing
- manipulate the world
- compare outcomes
- use evidence
- explain cause and effect
- transfer a pattern to a new situation

The child should not experience Science Lab as a fact-recall quiz.

## Curriculum strands

The production graph includes:

- inquiry and observation
- plants
- light and shadow
- water and weather
- material properties
- animals and habitats
- cause and effect

The graph is developmental and prerequisite-based. It does not claim formal standards alignment yet.

## World mechanics

- **Seed Bench** — plant parts, plant needs, growth sequence, growth-condition comparisons
- **Water Channel** — watering, flow direction, absorbency tests
- **Sun Prism** — light source, plant-light experiments, shadow investigations
- **Shadow Wall** — blocking light and changing shadow position/size
- **Weather Dial** — weather observation, comparison, simple patterns
- **Material Table** — properties, sorting, material-purpose matching, absorbency
- **Habitat Nests** — animal needs, habitat matching, body-part functions
- **Cause/Effect Machine** — prediction, sequencing, fair comparisons, transfer
- **Milo Inspect** — guided noticing and evidence selection

## Hidden placement

The first placement blueprint samples:

1. noticing details
2. prediction
3. plant needs
4. shadow cause
5. weather comparison
6. material testing
7. habitat reasoning
8. cause/effect sequencing
9. evidence-based explanation

Placement should establish provisional readiness only. It must never convert one diagnostic response into mastery.

## First playable slice

The native Science Lab should begin with the **Greenhouse**:

1. Milo notices a dormant seed bench
2. the child observes plant parts or conditions
3. the child predicts what the plant needs
4. water and/or light is physically routed to the plant
5. the plant visibly changes
6. the child compares the result with the prediction
7. successful investigation opens the route toward Weather Tower

The activity should be embedded in the environment, with no floating worksheet panel.

## Acceptance direction

- physical tap/manipulation on iPad
- strong foreground/player/gameplay/destination depth
- Valkyrie remains roughly 15–20% of the scene when composition permits
- Milo performs a functional inspection action
- incorrect predictions remain safe and useful
- environment reacts before any completion messaging
- science explanations stay short and concrete
